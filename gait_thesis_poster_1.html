01 Introduction

Why gait adaptation matters

Gait adaptations are the body's automatic adjustments that preserve balance and stability in challenging environments — stepping over an obstacle, turning, avoiding a hazard. When this adaptive capacity fails, the result is often a fall.

Falls are especially common among older adults and people with Parkinson's disease (PD), where reduced mobility and fear of movement compound over time and erode independence and quality of life.

02 Scientific Problem & Objective

From manual marking to an automated, phase-aware pipeline

Manually marking the moment of obstacle crossing in motion-capture data is slow and error-prone. Yet phase-specific detection — separating Pre-, Cross-, and Post-crossing phases — is essential to pinpoint exactly when and how instability emerges, and to guide targeted rehabilitation.

Research objectives

1. Develop and validate a robust algorithm to automatically detect crossing time and segment gait cycles into Pre-, Cross-, and Post-phases.

2. Compare gait adaptation strategies among young adults, elderly adults, and individuals with PD during an obstacle-crossing task, with emphasis on spatial gait parameters.

Experimental setup

  • Obstacle size — healthy controls: 60 × 30 × 10 cm; PD patients: 30 × 20 × 10 cm
  • VICON motion capture: 8 infrared cameras @ 100 Hz, full-body Plug-in Gait model
  • Walking path: figure-eight loop with two cones
  • Exclusions: cognitive impairment, recent injury, uncontrolled cardiac issues, other neurological disease

MATLAB processing pipeline

Inputs — pre-processed Vicon exports (GaitSummary, TimeSeriesGait, GaitEvents). Outputs — per-phase and cross-phase group comparisons and summaries, scoped to spatial parameters across the three groups.

STEP 1

Parameter-based targeting

Compute the median of all peak times across strides to define a Target Time.

STEP 2

Heel-strike alignment

Align Target Time to the nearest heel-strike to define the Cross Heel-Strike.

STEP 3

Phase segmentation

Automatically segment each trial into Pre-, Cross-, and Post-crossing phases.

STEP 4

Validation

Cross-check detected timing against manual VICON video review; accept if error ≤ 3 frames (~20 ms).

05 Discussion & Conclusion

Three distinct adaptation strategies

The developed algorithm accurately automated obstacle-crossing detection and revealed group-specific adaptation strategies, offering a scalable, phase-aware framework for future clinical and rehabilitation research.

Young Adults

Homogeneous & confident

Consistent clearance, strong propulsion, and narrow, symmetric steps across trials.

Older Adults

Cautious but efficient

Deliberately longer steps and slightly wider clearance while remaining largely symmetric — compensation without breakdown.

Parkinson's Disease

Reduced & inconsistent

Lower heel clearance, wider steps, and higher variability — a signature of reduced adaptive control and elevated trip risk.

06 Limitations & Future Work

Limitations

  • Small dataset limits statistical power
  • Analysis focused only on spatial parameters
  • Heel-strike-based detection is less reliable in PD gait
  • Different obstacle heights used across groups

Future implications

  • Expand the dataset for greater statistical power
  • Include temporal and dynamic gait variables
  • Combine EEG with gait data for cognitive-motor insight
  • Develop multi-marker detection for greater robustness
